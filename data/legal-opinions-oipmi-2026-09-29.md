# Legal opinions  
  
Opinion 1:  
  
Opinion: OIPM Remedies and Nostoi’s Right to Receive Agency Listing Feeds  
  
Prepared for: Custom AI Solutions (Pty) Ltd  
Re: Competition Commission OIPM remedies — enforceability of interoperability remedy against Prop Data  
  
1. Status of the remedies  
  
The Commission published its final OIPMI report and remedial actions on 31 July 2023. The remedies are binding on the affected parties unless contested before the Competition Tribunal. The remedial actions in relation to PropData are contained in Annexure 10 of the Final Report, as remedial actions under section 43D(1) of the Competition Act 89 of 1998.  
  
The remedies apply for a period of four years from the Effective Date, unless specified otherwise.  
  
There is no public record in the materials reviewed of any successful variation or appeal by Prop Data. Prop Data is required to submit compliance reports and is subject to the monitoring and non-compliance provisions set out below. The Commission has confirmed that implementation of OIPMI remedial actions “is still ongoing” as of August 2025. You should independently verify the current status of Prop Data’s compliance with the Commission directly (see contact details in section 6 below).  
  
2. Current text of the relevant remedies (PropData)  
  
The following are the material remedial action provisions in respect of Prop Data:  
  
PropData must provide interoperability at no fee for estate agents to feed listings to other platforms through API integration.  
  
The remedy as originally framed was an obligation owed to estate agents, not directly to third-party platforms. The operative language consistently describes the right as one that enables the estate agent to feed its listings to other platforms. The illustrative syndication selection screen in the Final Report confirms that the mechanism is a selection option available to the syndication software user (i.e. the estate agent), who ticks the platforms to which their listings should be pushed.  
  
Critically, however, the remedy contemplates that the feed will be delivered to “other online property classified platforms” through API integration, at no cost to estate agents or other online property classified platforms. That formulation suggests the Commission anticipated that third-party platforms would be the recipients of the feed, even though the trigger is exercised by the estate agent.  
  
3. Scope: does Nostoi have a direct right?  
  
In our view, the interoperability remedy does not create a directly enforceable right in favour of Nostoi against Prop Data. The obligation is structured as follows:  
  
· The right-holder is the estate agent (the licensed FFC holder who is Prop Data’s client).  
· The obligation is on Prop Data to implement the technical capability (API integration) so that the estate agent’s instruction to feed listings to a third platform is honoured at no fee.  
· The beneficiary of the feed can be an “other online property classified platform” — and the remedy expressly refers to “other online property classified platforms” as parties to whom no cost may be charged.  
  
The practical consequence is that the estate agent must elect Nostoi as a feed destination in the Prop Data syndication interface. Prop Data cannot lawfully refuse to implement that election, provided Nostoi falls within the scope of “other online property classified platform”.  
  
What the agency needs to do: The estate agent must select Nostoi in their syndication settings within Prop Data, and ideally instruct Prop Data in writing to enable the feed. You should draft this into your Platform Participation Agreement as a positive undertaking by the agency:  
  
“The Agency irrevocably instructs its syndication software provider (including, where applicable, Prop Data (Pty) Ltd) to feed the Agency’s listings to the Nostoi platform via API integration, and confirms that it does so pursuant to the interoperability remedy imposed by the Competition Commission in the OIPMI Final Report (Annexure 10).”  
  
This converts the remedy into a contractual right that the agency exercises on your behalf.  
  
4. Qualification: is Nostoi an “other platform”?  
  
The remedial actions do not contain a closed definition of “other online property classified platform”. The Commission’s general definition of an online intermediation platform is a platform that facilitates transactions between business users and consumers for the sale of goods, services or software, including online classified marketplaces.  
  
Nostoi is an AI-powered property advisory and discovery tool that presents listings to buyers. It is not a traditional classifieds portal, but it performs an intermediation function between estate agents (business users) and property buyers (consumers). The Commission’s concern was that the absence of interoperability hindered smaller competing platforms from attaining listings, which is the first crucial step in platform growth. That reasoning applies to Nostoi: without agency feeds, it cannot serve buyers, and it therefore cannot grow or compete.  
  
We recommend the following approach:  
  
1. Do not self-classify narrowly. In your communications with Prop Data and agencies, describe Nostoi as an “online property platform that receives listing feeds” rather than as something outside the scope of the remedy. The remedy’s language (“other online property classified platforms”) is broad enough to encompass a platform that displays listings to consumers.  
2. File a confirmation request. If Prop Data disputes your qualification, you can write to the Commission’s remedial action contact point (see below) requesting confirmation that Nostoi falls within the scope of the remedy. The Commission has shown willingness to engage on implementation issues.  
3. Align your PPA. Include a clause in your Platform Participation Agreement in which the agency warrants that it is entitled to feed its listings to Nostoi and that the feed complies with the OIPMI remedy. This shifts the compliance burden to the agency (who is the right-holder) and places you in the position of a recipient, not a claimant.  
  
5. Enforcement: practical route  
  
The remedial actions set out a structured non-compliance procedure:  
  
1. Engagement: If it appears Prop Data is not complying, the Commission may first engage with Prop Data to determine whether there is non-compliance.  
2. Written notice: The Commission may issue a written notice requiring compliance within 10 days.  
3. Tribunal application: If Prop Data fails to comply with the notice, the Commission may bring an application to the Competition Tribunal for an appropriate order.  
4. Variation: Prop Data may apply to the Commission, on good cause shown, to vary the remedial actions, and may appeal a refusal to the Tribunal within 20 days.  
  
Practical recommendation: If Prop Data declines or delays, the most effective route is:  
  
· First, have the agency instruct Prop Data in writing, copying you. This establishes that the agency has exercised its right under the remedy.  
· Second, if Prop Data still refuses, write to the Commission at inquiryremedialaction@compcom.co.za (the designated correspondence address for these remedial actions). Frame your letter as a third-party report of potential non-compliance with the interoperability remedy, attaching the agency’s instruction and Prop Data’s refusal. You are not making a formal complaint under the Competition Act; you are alerting the Commission to conduct that appears inconsistent with its remedial actions.  
· Third, if the Commission does not act, you could consider a formal complaint to the Commission under section 49B of the Competition Act. However, this is a slower route and requires you to establish that Prop Data’s refusal constitutes a prohibited practice (e.g. a refusal to deal that substantially lessens competition). Given that the remedy is already in place, the non-compliance route is likely faster.  
  
There is no public record of the Commission having brought a Tribunal application against Prop Data for non-compliance with the interoperability remedy. The Commission has generally preferred engagement and monitoring. This means that your best leverage is agency pressure plus Commission escalation, not direct litigation.  
  
6. Your own conduct: what you must not do  
  
The OIPMI findings and remedies contain principles that apply to platforms in your position as well. You must ensure that Nostoi does not replicate the conduct that was found to be anti-competitive:  
  
Prohibited practice What you must avoid  
Exclusive listing arrangements Do not require agencies to list exclusively on Nostoi or to cease listing on other platforms. The Commission found that exclusivity clauses and shareholding-linked contracts had a lock-in effect that harmed smaller platforms.  
Multi-year lock-in contracts Do not impose multi-year terms on agencies. The remedy explicitly prohibited Property24 and Private Property from entering into multi-year contracts with large agencies. Offer month-to-month or annual terms with termination for convenience.  
Charging for incoming listings Do not charge agencies a fee for sending their listings to Nostoi. The remedy prohibits charging for incoming listings, and the Commission found that the R500 per-office fee impeded rivals. Your value proposition should be on the buyer side (or via value-added services), not on feed ingestion.  
Preferential terms linked to equity If any agency holds an interest in Nostoi, do not link listing feeds or commercial terms to that shareholding. The Commission’s concern was that shareholder-agents received preferential terms that disadvantaged non-shareholder platforms.  
  
Your Platform Participation Agreement should contain an express non-exclusivity clause and a representations and warranties section in which the agency confirms that its participation in Nostoi does not breach any other agreement and that it is free to list on competing platforms.  
  
7. Accurate wording for agency outreach  
  
You may accurately state:  
  
“The Competition Commission’s Online Intermediation Platforms Market Inquiry (OIPMI) Final Report (July 2023) imposed a remedial action on Prop Data (and on Property24 and Private Property) requiring them to provide interoperability at no fee so that estate agents can feed their listings to other platforms through API integration. We will do the technical integration work at no cost to you. You simply need to instruct Prop Data to add Nostoi as a feed destination in your syndication settings.”  
  
You must NOT claim:  
  
· That the Commission “ordered Prop Data to feed Nostoi directly”. The order is directed at Prop Data’s obligation to enable the feed; the trigger is the agency’s election.  
· That you have an “enforceable right” against Prop Data. You do not, as a third party, have a direct claim. The agency does.  
· That the remedy is permanent. It applies for four years from the Effective Date.  
· That any specific agency is “required” to list on Nostoi. The remedy does not compel agencies to list on any particular platform; it removes the barriers to doing so.  
· That the Commission has “approved” Nostoi as a qualifying platform. No such approval exists. You should describe yourself as a recipient of feeds within the scope of the remedy, not as an approved or certified platform.  
  
8. Commercial sensitivity note  
  
Your disclosure that you lead a large eXp Realty organisation and that eXp is Nostoi’s launch inventory partner is relevant to how you frame the outreach. Several agencies you approach hold interests in Private Property through the EAPPC structure.  
  
The OIPMI findings were critical of the EAPPC shareholding arrangement and the preferential contracts that flowed from it. The remedy requires those contracts to transition to standard, non-preferential commercial terms. Your outreach should therefore emphasise:  
  
· Neutrality: Nostoi is not asking agencies to act against Private Property. The feed Nostoi needs is the same feed the agency already sends to every portal.  
· No conflict: eXp’s involvement in Nostoi is as an inventory partner, not as a platform that seeks to displace Private Property or Property24.  
· Compliance posture: Nostoi’s terms are designed to be compliant with the same principles the Commission applied to the incumbent platforms — non-exclusive, no incoming-listing fees, no multi-year lock-ins.  
  
If you wish to be maximally cautious, you could disclose in your outreach that eXp is a feed partner but has no shareholding in or control over Prop Data, Property24, or Private Property.  
  
9. Recommended next steps  
  
Step Action Timing  
1 Draft a one-page agency instruction letter that the agency can sign and send to Prop Data, electing Nostoi as a feed destination under the OIPMI remedy. Immediate  
2 Incorporate the instruction undertaking and non-exclusivity warranties into your Platform Participation Agreement. Immediate  
3 Write to the Commission at inquiryremedialaction@compcom.co.za requesting written confirmation that Nostoi falls within the scope of “other online property classified platforms” for the purposes of the Prop Data interoperability remedy. Within 2 weeks  
4 If Prop Data refuses after receiving the agency instruction, escalate to the Commission with a non-compliance report, attaching the agency instruction and Prop Data’s refusal. As needed  
5 Monitor the Commission’s OIPMI implementation updates (the Commission has said implementation is ongoing) for any variation or clarification affecting Prop Data. Ongoing  
  
This opinion is based on publicly available materials as at the date of preparation. It does not constitute legal advice and should be reviewed by your legal counsel before being relied upon in any contractual or regulatory context.

Opinion 2  
  
Here is an assessment of your position regarding the Competition Commission’s Online Intermediation Platforms Market Inquiry (OIPMI) remedies and how to leverage them for Nostoi.  
*Disclaimer: This is a strategic and practical analysis based on competition policy, not formal legal advice.*  
### 1. Status of the Remedies  
The interoperability and fee-removal remedies are binding and currently in force. According to the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition's recent implementation monitoring, the leading platforms have largely implemented the remedies (though Private Property’s compliance was previously flagged as "partial" during the transition phase). There have been no successful appeals overturning the core property portal remedies. The prohibition on incoming listing fees and the mandate for technical interoperability remain active compliance requirements for Property24, Private Property, and their proprietary or captive syndication tools (including Prop Data).  
### 2. Scope: Who holds the right?  
The remedy grants the right to the **estate agency**, not directly to Nostoi. The Commission’s intervention was designed to break vendor lock-in, meaning platforms and syndicators cannot technically block or financially penalize an estate agent for sending their own listing data to a third-party platform.  
Prop Data has no independent obligation to build a custom feed for Nostoi just because you ask; they have an obligation to facilitate the feed *when their client (the agency) instructs them to*.  
**Practical application:** You can and should draft this into your Platform Participation Agreement. Include a clause where the agency explicitly:  
 1. Authorizes Nostoi to receive their syndication feed.  
 2. Instructs their syndicator (e.g., Prop Data) to transmit the feed to Nostoi.  
 3. Appoints Nostoi as their technical agent to liaise with the syndicator to facilitate the connection.  
### 3. Qualification: Is Nostoi an "other platform"?  
Yes. The Commission’s definition of online intermediation platforms is broad, focusing on the function of connecting business users (agents) with consumers (buyers). You do not need to be a traditional classifieds portal to qualify. The remedies were explicitly designed to lower barriers to entry for innovative rivals, alternative distribution channels, and SME platforms. You do not need to register with the Commission to benefit from the market conditions created by the remedies.  
### 4. Enforcement route  
If Prop Data delays, throws up artificial technical barriers, or attempts to charge the agency a punitive fee for the outbound feed, the route is a **compliance complaint to the Competition Commission**.  
Because these remedies are the result of a finalized Market Inquiry, a breach is a direct violation of a binding remedial action. You do not need to initiate a years-long abuse of dominance case. You (or the agency) can report the non-compliance directly to the Commission's Market Conduct division, which monitors OIPMI implementation. Often, a formal letter from Nostoi to the syndicator cc’ing the Commission—referencing the OIPMI Final Report binding remedies—is enough to unblock the feed.  
### 5. Your own conduct (Remaining compliant)  
To ensure Nostoi stays cleanly on the right side of the Commission’s framework, avoid the specific behaviors condemned in the Final Report:  
 * **Do not charge for incoming listings:** Never charge an agency or a software provider a fee just to ingest their data.  
 * **Avoid multi-year lock-ins:** Keep your agency contracts flexible (e.g., month-to-month or easily cancellable), as long-term lock-ins were explicitly targeted by the Commission.  
 * **No exclusivity clauses:** Do not require agencies to list exclusively with Nostoi or restrict them from using competitors.  
 * **Fair pricing:** If you eventually monetize the agency side, ensure your pricing model does not aggressively discriminate against smaller independent agencies in favor of massive national brands.  
### 6. Accurate wording for agency outreach  
When pitching to agency principals, frame the integration as exercising their newly protected rights, rather than a battle between Nostoi and the portals.  
**What you MUST NOT claim:**  
 * "The Competition Commission ordered Prop Data to give Nostoi your listings." (Inaccurate; the order empowers the *agency*).  
 * "Prop Data/Private Property are acting illegally." (Unnecessary and risks antagonizing agencies with ownership stakes).  
**Accurate wording you MAY use:**  
> *"Under the Competition Commission's 2023 Market Inquiry remedies, your agency has the right to direct your syndication provider (like Prop Data) to feed your listings to alternative platforms without facing technical barriers or extra fees. Because you already syndicate your listings, there is no extra operational work for your team to join Nostoi. If you sign our participation mandate, we will manage the technical integration with your syndicator on your behalf to ensure your listings flow freely to our AI advisor."*  
>

Opinion 3  
  
I need current, primary-source information on the Competition Commission’s OIPM remedies for property platforms. I’ll start by locating the final report, the formal remedies order, any subsequent variations, consent agreements, or enforcement actions.**Short opinion (as at late September 2026)**  
The Competition Commission’s binding remedial actions under the Online Intermediation Platforms Market Inquiry (OIPMI) Final Report and Decision (published 31 July 2023; Effective Date ≈ 31 July 2023) require PropData (and the leading portals) to enable free interoperability so that estate agencies can feed listings to other platforms. They do **not** give a third-party platform such as Nostoi an independent, enforceable right to compel PropData (or the portals) to supply feeds. The right sits with the estate agent / agency (the business user). Nostoi can use the remedies practically by (a) completing any required API integration with PropData and (b) having agencies elect Nostoi in PropData’s selection interface (or instruct PropData in writing). Whether Nostoi strictly qualifies as an “online property classified platform” under the PropData remedy definition is open to argument and is best resolved by practical engagement or a Commission compliance query rather than assumed.  
I have seen no public record that the PropData, Property24 or Private Property interoperability / incoming-fee remedies were varied, set aside on appeal, or replaced by consent orders that dilute the core obligations. (Booking.com settled its appeal in 2024 on different issues; the property remedies appear to have run their course under the four-year duration.) Public monitoring detail after the initial compliance reports is sparse.  
## 1. Status of the remedies  
The remedies are decisions under s 43D of the Competition Act and were binding from the Effective Date. Key timelines for PropData were 12 months (i.e. by ≈ 31 July 2024). Duration is four years from the Effective Date unless varied. Platforms could apply to the Commission (and thereafter the Tribunal) to vary on good cause / significant change in market circumstances. No public variation or successful challenge specific to the PropData feed-out / no-fee / no-conditions obligations has been located. Property24 and Private Property had parallel (and in some respects earlier) obligations to stop charging incoming-feed fees and to enable inbound APIs; those also appear to remain in force for the residual term.  
## 2. Exact text of the PropData remedies (Annexure 10)  
Relevant operative clauses (paraphrased only where the source extraction is fragmentary; the Commission’s published Annexure 10 is the authoritative text):  
* PropData must, within twelve Months of the Effective Date, put in place the functionality to feed out listings from PropData to other **online property classified platforms** through Application Programme Interface (API) integration **at no cost to estate agents or other online property classified platforms**. PropData must not place conditions on API access and integration by other online property classified platforms.  
* PropData must, within twelve Months of the Effective Date, offer PropData Syndication software users the ability to select which online property classified platforms (that have completed API integration with the PropData platform) they want to feed their listings to.  
* PropData must, within twelve Months of the Effective Date, clearly and unambiguously notify estate agent clients of PropData, in writing, of the functionality to feed their listings to those online property classified platforms that have completed API integration.  
* Duration: four years from the Effective Date.  
* Monitoring: compliance report + confirmatory affidavit within 12 months; further information on request.  
* Non-compliance process: Commission engagement → written notice requiring compliance within 10 Days → application to the Tribunal for an appropriate order.  
* Variation possible on good cause shown.  
**Definition** (PropData remedies): “Online property classified platforms” means web or app-based platforms that provide estate agents the ability to list their properties for sale or rental and for consumers to search and review properties from a wide variety of estate agents.  
Parallel obligations on Property24 / Private Property required them to cease charging fees for incoming feeds from other syndication software / platforms and to make inbound APIs available (Property24 immediately / early; Private Property by end-2023 for the fee cessation).  
## 3. Scope – who holds the right?  
The remedies are structured around the **estate agent / agency** (business user) choosing where its listings are fed. PropData must:  
* enable free API feed-out to any qualifying “other online property classified platform” that has integrated, and  
* give its users a selection mechanism (and notify them of it).  
There is no free-standing right for a third-party platform to demand the feed independently of the agency’s selection or instruction. In practice:  
* Nostoi (or its technical partner) completes API integration with PropData (PropData may not impose conditions).  
* The agency then selects Nostoi in the PropData interface or issues a written instruction to PropData to include Nostoi in its feed.  
* You can (and should) draft this election / instruction into your Platform Participation Agreement so the agency’s signature both authorises the feed and evidences the request that triggers PropData’s obligation.  
If PropData refuses a properly integrated platform or imposes conditions / delays after an agency election, that is the non-compliance scenario contemplated by the remedy.  
## 4. Does Nostoi qualify?  
The definition is geared to traditional classifieds portals (agents list; consumers search and review a wide variety of listings). Nostoi is described as an AI property advisor that maintains a live listing book, presents a curated small set of homes with evidence of fit, and does not rank by payment. It is two-sided but not a classic classifieds portal in the Property24 mould.  
A purposive reading (the Inquiry’s concern was denial of listings to rival platforms that could compete for agency inventory) supports treating a live-listing AI advisor that receives agency feeds and surfaces properties to buyers as within the spirit of “other online property classified platforms.” A strict textual reading leaves room for PropData (or the Commission) to argue otherwise. Practical steps that reduce risk:  
* Position Nostoi, in correspondence and any integration request, as an online platform that receives agency listings for the purpose of enabling consumers to search/review properties.  
* Complete the technical integration and obtain clear agency elections.  
* If refused, raise a compliance query with the Commission (inquiryremedialaction@compcom.co.za or the market-inquiry monitoring channel) rather than assuming a private right of action.  
No filing or formal “qualification” process is prescribed in the remedies.  
## 5. Enforcement route if PropData declines or delays  
1. Agency (or Nostoi with the agency’s written authority) requests integration / selection in writing, citing the remedy.  
2. If refused or conditioned, escalate to the Commission (compliance / non-compliance process in the remedy itself). The Commission can engage, issue a 10-day compliance notice, and ultimately apply to the Tribunal.  
3. A formal complaint under the Act’s ordinary enforcement provisions remains available but is slower; the remedy’s built-in monitoring and Tribunal application route is the more direct path contemplated by the Inquiry.  
Public information on post-2024 enforcement of these specific property remedies is limited. The Commission has actively monitored and settled other OIPMI remedies (e.g., Booking.com) and has continued broader digital-platform guidance work (final Guidance Note published February 2026). There is no published record of Tribunal proceedings or variation applications that have diluted the PropData feed-out obligations.  
## 6. Constraints on Nostoi’s own contracting  
The remedial actions bind only the named parties (Property24, Private Property, PropData, etc.). As a new entrant Nostoi is not subject to those specific orders. However, the Inquiry’s reasoning flags the following as features that impede competition:  
* charging fees for incoming listings from other platforms / syndication software,  
* multi-year lock-in contracts that commit agency budgets and crowd out rivals,  
* any arrangement that effectively prevents agencies from feeding listings to other platforms.  
To stay unambiguously on the right side of the Commission’s findings (and the 2026 Guidance Note on online intermediation platforms), avoid exclusivity that prevents agencies feeding elsewhere, multi-year non-cancellable commitments that lock budgets, and any incoming-feed fees if/when Nostoi achieves significant scale. Short, terminable participation terms and free inbound feeds are consistent with the Inquiry’s pro-competitive direction.  
## 7. Accurate wording for agency outreach  
You may say, in substance:  
“Under the Competition Commission’s binding remedial actions arising from the 2023 Online Intermediation Platforms Market Inquiry, PropData is required to enable estate agencies to feed their listings, at no cost and without conditions, to other online property classified platforms that have completed API integration with PropData. Agencies using PropData may select those platforms (or instruct PropData in writing). Nostoi will complete the necessary technical integration and will handle the work required on our side so that, once your agency elects to include us, the feed can flow under the Commission’s remedies.”  
Do **not** claim:  
* that Nostoi has an independent statutory right to the feed,  
* that the Commission has ordered PropData to supply Nostoi specifically,  
* that agencies are obliged to feed Nostoi, or  
* that the remedies require divestiture of Private Property interests as a condition of dealing with you (the EAPPC/Private Property shareholding issue was the subject of a separate Tribunal recommendation; it is not a condition of the interoperability remedy).  
Emphasise that the feed you seek is the same syndication feed agencies already send to other platforms; you are not asking them to act against any platform in which they (or their funders) hold an interest.  
## Practical next steps  
1. Request API documentation / integration from PropData in writing, referencing the Annexure 10 obligations and stating that Nostoi is an online platform that will receive agency listings for consumer search/review.  
2. Prepare a short agency election letter / clause for your Platform Participation Agreement that both authorises the feed and constitutes the request to PropData.  
3. If integration or selection is refused or delayed after a clear agency request, write to the Commission’s remedial-action monitoring address with the paper trail and ask for a compliance engagement.  
4. Keep your own contracts short-term, non-exclusive on the feed side, and free of incoming-listing fees.

Opinion 4  
  
## Short opinion  
  
**Yes—conditionally.** The OIPMI remedy creates a strong regulatory route for Nostoi to receive an agency’s listings through PropData **at no fee**, but the operative right is framed as one exercised **“on request by client agencies”** rather than as a free-standing direct entitlement of Nostoi against PropData. In practice, Nostoi should secure a written agency authorisation/mandate and submit it jointly with the agency to PropData.  
  
The binding published remedy is broad enough to cover a genuinely consumer-facing online property platform such as Nostoi, even if it is an AI-led advisory/search product rather than a conventional “Property24 mould” classifieds site. That conclusion is not risk-free because the remedies do not define “third-party property classifieds” or prescribe a certification process; your product and contracting should therefore make clear that Nostoi displays and enables discovery of agency property listings for South African property seekers, while preserving the agency’s control of its data and leads.  
  
This is a commercial-regulatory opinion based on the published OIPMI decision and publicly available material, not litigation advice on a specific disputed refusal.  
  
## Current published remedies  
  
The Final Report and Decision was gazetted on 28 July 2023, with the report made available from 31 July 2023. The defined “Effective Date” is the date of Gazette publication, so the relevant 12-month implementation period ran from 28 July 2023.[1][2]  
  
The relevant current *published* text appears in Annexure 10 to the Final Report. The key provisions are:

[2][3] |  
| **Property24** | Property24 must “cease charging a fee to feed-in agency listings from other syndication software providers and online property classified platforms.” | From the Effective Date. [3] |

The Final Report’s plain-language summary is especially helpful for outreach: it says that **Property24, Private Property and PropData must provide interoperability at no fee for estate agents to feed listings to other platforms**, and that Property24 and Private Property must stop charging for incoming listings and end multi-year contracts with large agencies.[2]  
  
### Are they in force today?  
  
On the public material located, the **published OIPMI remedial actions remain the governing public text**. I did not locate a public variation decision, Tribunal order, appeal ruling, or consent agreement specifically replacing the Property24/Private Property/PropData property-feed remedies.  
  
That is important, but it is not the same as proving that no variation or private implementation protocol exists. Annexure 10 permits a respondent to apply to the Commission for a variation on good cause, and then to apply to the Tribunal if dissatisfied with the Commission’s decision. It also permits deadline extensions by agreement between the relevant parties.[3]  
  
Accordingly, before making an absolute public assertion that the original wording remains unmodified, request written confirmation from the Commission’s OIPMI remedial-action team of:  
  
- Whether PropData, Property24, or Private Property sought or received a variation, extension, suspension, or alternative implementation arrangement;  
- The current compliance status of each property-classifieds remedy;  
- The current contact/process for a client agency and a third-party property platform requesting an outgoing PropData feed;  
- Whether there is a standard agency authorisation form, API specification, onboarding requirement, or data-security protocol.  
  
The published remedial-action correspondence address is `inquiryremedialaction@compcom.co.za`.[3]  
  
## Does Nostoi have a feed right?  
  
### Legal and practical answer  
  
The better reading is:  
  
1. **The agency holds the trigger right.**    
   The published formulation is not “PropData must supply any third-party platform that asks.” It requires interoperable functionality/API **on request by client agencies**. That language puts the agency—not Nostoi—at the centre of the request.[2][3]  
  
2. **Nostoi is the designated destination/technical recipient.**    
   Once a PropData client agency asks to syndicate its own listings to Nostoi, the remedy is directed at removing the technical and fee barrier to that agency doing so. The agency should be entitled to nominate Nostoi as the receiving platform, subject to ordinary, proportionate technical and security requirements.  
  
3. **“At no fee” is most naturally read as no interoperability/feed-out fee.**    
   PropData should not charge the agency or Nostoi a fee for the remedy-required ability to feed listings to the designated third-party property platform. The remedy does not necessarily forbid fees for genuinely optional services outside interoperability—for example, bespoke software development, premium data-enrichment products, or abnormal support work—but those cannot be used as a disguised condition or toll for ordinary feed-out functionality.  
  
4. **It is not a compulsory licence over listing data.**    
   The remedy removes the platform/syndication bottleneck; it does not displace the agency’s rights, its seller mandates, POPIA obligations, copyright/database rights, or listing-quality controls. Nostoi should obtain the agency’s express licence/authorisation to receive, store, process, display and refresh the feed, and should provide takedown and correction mechanisms.  
  
### Recommended agency authorisation  
  
Your Platform Participation Agreement should include a short, separately executable **Listing Feed Authorisation and Direction**. The agency should state, in substance:  
  
> The Agency authorises and directs PropData (and any relevant listing-management or syndication provider used by the Agency) to provide Nostoi / Custom AI Solutions (Pty) Ltd with a continuing electronic feed of the Agency’s active property listings and associated listing data for display and search on Nostoi, subject to this Agreement and the Agency’s listing mandates. The Agency confirms that it has authority to give this direction and grants Nostoi a non-exclusive, revocable licence to receive, process, index, display, update and remove that content for the operation of Nostoi. The Agency requests that the feed be enabled under the interoperability functionality required by the Competition Commission’s OIPMI remedial actions, without an interoperability or feed-out fee.  
  
Add an annexure that identifies:  
  
- Agency legal name, trading name, PropData client/account number, and authorised signatory;  
- Nostoi legal entity, technical contact, API/XML endpoint or SFTP details;  
- Territories and listing types;  
- The feed fields requested;  
- Update frequency and deletion/takedown requirements;  
- Brand, attribution, logo and agent-contact-display rules;  
- Whether leads go to the listing agent/agency directly;  
- POPIA roles and data-security commitments;  
- A clear statement that the agency may revoke authorisation and require removal of its listings.  
  
Use the word **“directs”** rather than merely “consents.” The practical objective is to make clear to PropData that its own client has made the outgoing syndication request contemplated by the remedy.  
  
## Does Nostoi qualify?  
  
Nostoi has a **good but not conclusive** argument that it is within the intended recipient class.  
  
The OIPMI describes the problem as a barrier to rival **property classifieds platforms** obtaining listings and refers to the ability of estate agencies to share listings with “other classifieds.” The inquiry’s concern was that a property platform needs a broad listing base to offer useful search and comparison, and that technical restrictions imposed by leading platforms and PropData prevented rivals from acquiring that inventory.[2]  
  
Nostoi’s facts fit the competitive concern if it:  
  
- Is a South African consumer-facing digital service enabling buyers and renters to discover, compare and enquire about property listings;  
- Displays agency-authorised listings and material listing information;  
- Sends leads or enquiries to the listing agency/agent;  
- Does not sell its own competing inventory;  
- Uses relevance/fit rather than paid placement as its consumer-ranking logic;  
- Does not require agency exclusivity or prevent agencies from listing elsewhere.  
  
The fact that Nostoi is an AI advisor is not, by itself, a reason to exclude it. A narrow interpretation limited to clones of traditional listing portals would undercut the inquiry’s stated objective of enabling competing platforms to develop and obtain listings. The Commission’s own scope described online intermediation platforms as B2C services facilitating transactions between business users and consumers, including online classifieds.[1]  
  
### Residual qualification risk  
  
The weak point is that the specific remedy uses the phrase **“third-party property classifieds.”** Nostoi is strongest if its live product visibly performs the core classifieds/intermediation functions: property search, listing presentation, agency identification, property enquiry/lead routing, and a route to the authoritative listing/agency.  
  
Avoid positioning Nostoi to PropData as merely:  
  
- A private internal research tool;  
- A buyer-side data analytics product;  
- A scraping, data-resale, or lead-generation service detached from agency listings;  
- A general AI assistant that happens to consume property data.  
  
Instead, describe it accurately as:  
  
> “A South African, consumer-facing online property discovery and advisory platform that enables buyers and renters to search and assess agency-authorised residential listings and connect with the listing agency or agent.”  
  
There appears to be **no published registration, filing, or pre-approval procedure** for an “other platform.” The sensible route is a written eligibility/compliance enquiry to the Commission and a joint, agency-led onboarding request to PropData.  
  
## Enforcement and escalation  
  
### First: make a clean agency-led request  
  
Do not begin with a complaint. Send PropData a concise joint request containing:  
  
- The signed agency authorisation/direction;  
- A plain description of Nostoi;  
- The technical endpoint and a request for the normal API/XML feed or documented onboarding process;  
- A statement that the agency requests access under the OIPMI interoperability remedy;  
- A request for confirmation of any proportionate technical/security onboarding requirements;  
- A short response deadline, for example 10 business days.  
  
Ask PropData to identify in writing, if it refuses or conditions the request, the exact reason and the published term on which it relies. This creates the record needed for the Commission.  
  
### Second: seek a compliance enquiry  
  
If PropData delays, demands a feed-out charge, insists that Nostoi is ineligible without a principled basis, or offers only an impractical substitute, email the OIPMI remedial-action address with the agency copied. Ask the Commission to treat it as a **compliance enquiry concerning Annexure 10**, not initially as a broad new competition complaint.  
  
Attach:  
  
- The signed agency direction;  
- The correspondence and any pricing/conditions demanded;  
- A one-page explanation of Nostoi’s consumer-facing property-platform function;  
- Evidence that the agency is a PropData client;  
- The technical requirements and Nostoi’s security/POPIA assurances;  
- A requested outcome: confirmation of the applicable process and direction that the relevant interoperability functionality be made available without fee.  
  
The published compliance mechanism is meaningful: the Commission may first engage the firm to determine whether there is non-compliance; if it considers the firm non-compliant, it may issue a written notice requiring compliance within the stated period; failure to comply with that notice can lead to a Tribunal application for an appropriate order.[3]  
  
### Third: consider a formal complaint or legal relief  
  
A formal complaint under the Competition Act becomes more relevant where the conduct goes beyond remedy implementation—for example, coordinated refusal, exclusionary tying, discriminatory access, or unreasonable terms designed to preserve the incumbents’ position.  
  
For your immediate commercial objective, however, an agency-led compliance escalation should be faster, less adversarial and more closely tied to the exact remedial text.  
  
### What has been publicly enforced?  
  
The publicly accessible material supports the following, but no more:  
  
- The Commission published and gazetted binding remedial actions and built monitoring/non-compliance provisions into Annexure 10.[1][3]  
- Public commentary in late 2023 reported that Property24 had already removed the approximately R500 feed-in fee for users using third-party vendors to feed into its application.[4]  
- I did not find a published Commission enforcement decision, Tribunal compliance order, or public PropData-specific compliance determination resolving an agency’s request for an outgoing listing feed.  
  
That absence does **not** mean the remedy is unenforceable. It means you should avoid saying that the Commission has already compelled PropData to provide feeds to platforms like Nostoi unless you obtain a written Commission confirmation or a specific public decision.  
  
## Nostoi’s own conduct  
  
The remedies are formally directed at the named respondents, not at Nostoi. Nonetheless, their reasoning provides a sensible competition-law design guide.  
  
### Terms to avoid  
  
Avoid contractual terms that could recreate the restrictive features identified by the Inquiry:  
  
- **Agency exclusivity:** do not require an agency to give Nostoi exclusive listing rights or to stop listing on Property24, Private Property, agency sites, other portals, or competing AI/property platforms.  
- **De facto exclusivity:** avoid volume rebates, ranking benefits, lead guarantees, data-access penalties, or preferential treatment conditional on limiting use of other platforms.  
- **Multi-year lock-ins:** use short, terminable agreements—ideally monthly or annual terms—with practical exit rights. If you need a longer technical or commercial term, ensure it is genuinely optional and does not restrict multi-homing.  
- **Incoming-feed charges:** do not charge agencies for the ordinary technical receipt of authorised listing feeds. If Nostoi later charges for optional premium services, separate them clearly from basic feed ingestion and do not make them a condition of being listed.  
- **Paid ranking:** do not allow payment to determine listing ranking, especially given your stated product proposition. If any advertising is offered, label it prominently and keep it separate from the advisor’s relevance results.  
- **Data restrictions:** do not impose unnecessary restrictions preventing an agency from using its own listing data elsewhere, and do not use agency data to build competing agency services without a clear contractual licence.  
  
### Positive commitments worth adopting  
  
Your agreement and public policy can affirm that:  
  
- Agencies remain free to list on any other property platform;  
- Participation is non-exclusive;  
- Nostoi does not require a multi-year commitment;  
- Core feed onboarding is free;  
- Results are not ranked by payment;  
- The agency retains control over listing activation, correction and removal;  
- Buyer leads are transparently routed to the relevant listing agency/agent;  
- Commercial features, if introduced, cannot affect organic/advisory ranking.  
  
That stance does not merely reduce legal risk. It makes Nostoi’s outreach more credible to principals who may also have an interest in Private Property: you are not asking them to withdraw, boycott, or act against any platform they part-own. You are asking them to use their own existing listing-management feed to make the same agency-authorised listings available to an additional, non-exclusive consumer-discovery channel.  
  
## Suggested outreach wording  
  
### Recommended wording  
  
> Nostoi is a consumer-facing South African property discovery and advisory platform. We would like to display your agency’s existing authorised listings and route buyer enquiries to your team.    
>  
> The Competition Commission’s 2023 Online Intermediation Platforms Market Inquiry remedial actions require PropData, Property24 and Private Property to provide interoperability, at no fee, so that estate agencies can feed their listings to other property platforms. The PropData remedy is designed to be activated by a request from its client agency.    
>  
> If you choose to participate, you would authorise Nostoi as an additional, non-exclusive destination for your existing listings feed. Nostoi will do the technical onboarding work, will not charge an incoming-listing fee, and will not require you to reduce or cease your use of any existing portal or platform.    
>  
> Nostoi’s property recommendations are based on buyer fit and listing evidence, not payment for ranking.  
  
This tracks the published remedy without overstating it.[2][3]  
  
### Wording to avoid  
  
Do **not** say:  
  
- “PropData is legally obliged to give Nostoi its complete database.”  
- “Nostoi has a statutory right to every agency’s listings.”  
- “The Commission ordered PropData to supply us directly.”  
- “PropData may not impose any terms, technical controls, data-security requirements, or integration process.”  
- “Your agency must participate.”  
- “The Commission requires agencies to list on Nostoi.”  
- “Private Property or Property24 is acting unlawfully” unless you have evidence and legal advice on the specific conduct.  
- “The remedies were implemented exactly as published” unless you first obtain confirmation that no variation, extension or substituted implementation terms apply.  
  
The accurate distinction is: **the Commission required interoperability enabling an estate agency, on its request, to feed its listings to other platforms at no fee; Nostoi seeks to be the agency-authorised receiving platform.**  
  
## Recommended next steps  
  
1. Have competition counsel obtain a written compliance-status confirmation from the Commission, specifically naming PropData, Property24 and Private Property.  
2. Finalise Nostoi’s Platform Participation Agreement with a stand-alone feed authorisation/direction and data-processing schedule.  
3. Build the product and technical packet that demonstrates Nostoi is a bona fide consumer-facing property discovery platform: live URL or demo, listing display, lead routing, ranking policy, security controls and removal process.  
4. Approach each agency as the principal: secure its signed direction first, then send a joint request to PropData.  
5. Preserve a complete written record of any refusal, delay, fee demand or restrictive condition.  
6. If a request is blocked, send a focused OIPMI remedial-action compliance enquiry before escalating to a formal competition complaint.  
  
The central practical point is that the OIPMI remedy is a **route through the agency’s authorisation**, not a mechanism for Nostoi to demand unconsented data directly from PropData. Properly structured, that is still commercially valuable: it gives agencies a competition-law-backed basis to add Nostoi as a no-fee, non-exclusive syndication destination without disturbing their existing portal arrangements.

Opinion 5  
  
Nostoi and the OIPMI property-listing remedies  
Focused opinion | 29 September 2026  
  
Conclusion  
Nostoi has a credible, but not automatic, route to ask for agency-authorised listings through PropData, Fusion or PropCtrl. The Commission’s 2023 schedules require those syndication systems to make API-based feeds available to other online property-classified platforms, at no charge, and to let agency users choose among platforms that have completed API integration. The agency is the practical trigger: the report describes interoperability as being “on request by client agencies,” and the schedules require an agency-facing platform-selection function. The text does not give Nostoi a unilateral right to pull any agency’s listings, nor an express private cause of action against a feed provider. 2 3  
  
Nostoi’s AI-led interface is not disqualified just because it is not a conventional classifieds portal. The schedule uses a functional definition. Nostoi is a plausible fit if agencies can submit listings to it and buyers can search for and review listings from a range of agencies. Whether Nostoi’s curated, conversational presentation is enough has not been decided by the Commission, so do not represent eligibility as officially confirmed. 3  
  
What the schedules say—and their present status  
The relevant text is in Annexure 10 to the Commission’s July 2023 Final Report and Decision. The PropData schedule says:  
  
 “PropData must, within twelve Months of the Effective Date, put in place the functionality to feed out listings from PropData to other online property classified platforms through Application Programme Interface (API) integration at no cost to estate agents or other online property classified platforms. PropData must not place conditions on API access and integration by other online property classified platforms.” 3   
  
It separately requires PropData to offer its syndication-software users a way to select which already-integrated online property-classified platforms receive their listings, and to notify its agency clients in writing. The schedule defines those platforms as web- or app-based platforms that let estate agents list properties for sale or rent and let consumers search and review properties from a wide variety of agents. 3  
  
The remedies are not identical for all three providers:  
  
	•	PropData: the quoted API feed-out/no-fee/no-conditions obligation; agency selection and notice obligations. Its schedule does not impose the same incoming-listing-fee remedy as the portals. 3  
	•	Private Property/Fusion: Private Property had to stop charging, by the end of 2023, for agency listings fed in from other syndication software or property-classified platforms. It also had to provide feed-out from Fusion by API at no cost to agents or receiving platforms, without conditions on API access, and provide agency selection and notice. 3  
	•	Property24/PropCtrl: Property24 had to stop charging for feed-in of agency listings from third-party syndication software or other property-classified platforms from the Effective Date. It also had to provide PropCtrl feed-out by API at no cost, without access conditions, and provide agency selection and notice. 3  
  
Each schedule says the remedies apply for four years from its defined Effective Date. The definition ties that date to publication of the Final Report and Decision in the Gazette. The Gazette notice is dated 28 July 2023 and says the report would be available online from 31 July. On that basis, the twelve-month implementation deadline fell around late July 2024 and the four-year term runs to around late July 2027; the three-day publication-date difference does not change the present conclusion. 1 3  
  
As of 29 September 2026, the schedules remain in force on their face, and I found no public record of a property-specific variation, appeal, replacement order, or Tribunal enforcement order. That is not proof none exists: the schedules allow a respondent to seek variation from the Commission for good cause, permit a further Tribunal application in specified circumstances, and allow deadlines to be extended by agreement. The Commission’s 2026 general Guidance Note does not itself amend these named 2023 schedules; it is non-binding guidance for future platform-conduct assessments. 3 6  
  
There is a legal-status qualification. These are Commission remedial-action schedules under section 43D(1), not a Tribunal order or a consent order that Nostoi can enforce itself. The schedules expressly provide for Commission-led enforcement: the Commission may engage the respondent, give written notice to comply within ten business days, and then apply to the Tribunal for an appropriate order. The 2026 Competition Appeal Court decision in African Rainbow Capital v Competition Commission confirms that only the Tribunal can order divestiture under section 43D(2) read with section 60(2)(c). That case concerned divestiture, not the API remedies; it does not decide the validity or enforcement of these interoperability clauses. 3 4 5  
  
I found no public, property-specific account of enforcement since 2023 that confirms whether PropData’s API is fully implemented or identifies a Commission finding of non-compliance. A 2026 practitioner update says the Commission continues to monitor OIPMI remedies generally, including classifieds, but does not report the status of PropData’s property API. The safest description is therefore: the requirements are still operative on the published record, but public sources do not establish actual implementation or a direct Nostoi entitlement. 7  
  
Agency instruction and Nostoi’s qualification  
The report’s operative summary says PropCtrl, Fusion and PropData must provide interoperability “via an API on request by client agencies for no fee.” The detailed schedules do not prescribe a special form of request or require a particular contract. They do require a selection feature for agencies, once the destination platform has completed API integration. A signed agency instruction in Nostoi’s Platform Participation Agreement is therefore useful evidence of the request and listing authority, but it cannot by itself compel a provider to treat Nostoi as eligible or bypass technical integration. 2 3  
  
A practical clause could say:  
  
 Agency feed instruction. The Agency instructs and authorises its syndication provider, including PropData, Fusion or PropCtrl, to make the Agency’s eligible Listings available to Nostoi through the applicable API, and elects Nostoi as a feed destination wherever the provider’s selection functionality permits. This instruction is non-exclusive and applies only to Listings the Agency is authorised to syndicate. The Agency may withdraw or amend it for a Listing. Nostoi will complete the technical integration and will not charge the Agency or provider for receiving the feed. This clause records the Agency’s instruction; it does not represent that the Commission has individually determined Nostoi’s eligibility.   
  
That clause should sit alongside explicit rights to display listing text, photographs, agency identity and contact details, and any seller-mandate or privacy permissions needed for the feed. Ask the agency principal to submit the corresponding instruction to its vendor as well, and retain proof of the request and the vendor’s response.  
  
Nostoi’s best eligibility case is that its website/app lets multiple agencies supply listings and lets buyers find, compare and review those listings through natural-language requirements. The definition does not require a standard search-results page or prohibit advisory features. The risk is that a provider or the Commission could say a curated advisory service does not itself let agencies “list” properties or consumers “search and review” a wide range of agencies’ listings. Make the agency and listing source clear on each property, preserve discoverability of the underlying listing, and ask the Commission for written clarification before making a categorical claim. The remedy schedules do not establish a registration or pre-clearance process. 3  
  
Enforcement and Nostoi’s own terms  
If a provider refuses, delays, charges, or says Nostoi is not an eligible destination, first obtain a written response and record the agency’s instruction, proposed feed scope, API specifications or integration steps, fee demand, and delay. Then write to the Commission at the address specified in the schedule, inquiryremedialaction@compcom.co.za, asking it to confirm the applicable clause and consider the schedule’s compliance process. The schedule contemplates a Commission inquiry, a written ten-business-day compliance notice, and then a Tribunal application; it does not give Nostoi a direct power to issue that notice. 3  
  
The 2023 schedules bind the named respondents, not Nostoi. They do not themselves prohibit Nostoi from using exclusivity, multi-year contracts, or charging agencies. But those practices were among the features the Commission identified as impeding competition. For a clean position, use non-exclusive, readily terminable agency terms; do not charge a feed-in or API-access fee to receive agency inventory; do not make presence on Nostoi conditional on leaving another portal; and keep any paid services separate from organic matching. These are prudent design choices, not conclusions that the OIPMI order directly binds Nostoi. 2 3 6  
  
Suggested outreach wording  
 “The Competition Commission’s 2023 OIPMI remedial schedules require PropData, Fusion and PropCtrl to support API feeds to other online property-classified platforms at no charge, and to let agency users choose integrated destinations. The schedules define the relevant platform category by what it enables agencies and consumers to do. Nostoi lets buyers find and review agency listings through an AI property-advisory experience; we consider that a credible fit with the definition, although the Commission has not individually ruled on Nostoi. We will handle the technical integration and would welcome your written instruction to your syndication provider to include Nostoi as an additional destination. We are not asking you to stop feeding any existing portal or change any shareholding or industry relationship.”   
  
You may accurately say the Commission required free API interoperability for qualifying platforms, subject to agency choice and integration. Do not say “the Commission gave Nostoi a right to your feed,” “PropData must send every agency’s listings to Nostoi,” or “Nostoi has been approved by the Commission.” Also do not imply that the remedies require an agency to divert or remove listings from Property24 or Private Property. 2 3  
  
This is a focused research opinion based on public materials, not a formal South African legal opinion or substitute for advice from competition counsel. The key unresolved point is Nostoi’s classification under the functional definition; counsel or the Commission should confirm that before the language is used as an unqualified legal assertion.

Opinion 6:  
# Nostoi: listing-feed access under the OIPMI remedies  
**Qualified research opinion — 29 September 2026**  
**My conclusion:** The remedies provide a substantial basis for seeking **an agency-authorised, fee-free interoperability connection**. They should be used to support an agency’s instruction to its syndication provider—not presented as an unconditional right for Nostoi to obtain every agency’s listings.  
There is evidence that the remedies have delivered access to competing property platforms. However, **I could not verify a consolidated, currently operative set of terms for all three respondents, including every subsequent variation or order**. I would therefore approve the measured outreach wording below, but not an unqualified legal demand asserting that Prop Data must supply Nostoi’s requested feed free of every possible charge.  
## 1. Status and the remedy wording  
The Commission’s **July 2023 published summary**, printed page 8, states:  
“Property24, Private Property and PropData must provide interoperability at no fee for estate agents to feed listings to other platforms.”   
It separately requires Property24 and Private Property to **“cease charging for incoming listings”**. That addresses their charge for receiving listings from external syndication software; it is not a requirement that all portal advertising services become free.   
These were remedial decisions, not merely voluntary industry recommendations. The Competition Act provides for Commission remedial action following a market inquiry and appeals against those decisions: sections 43D–43F. Conversion into a Tribunal consent order is not a prerequisite for every inquiry remedy to have legal effect.   
**The subsequent position I could establish is:**  

| Respondent | Verified position and remaining qualification |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Property24 | The September 2023 parliamentary record reported that it accepted the findings. The Commission’s later implementation presentation reports implementation across leading platforms, with exceptions for Apple and Private Property—not Property24. This supports implementation, but is not a clause-by-clause statement of its current terms. |
| Private Property | The later Commission presentation expressly describes implementation as partial. It does not identify which obligations remain outstanding. That does not establish that its interoperability obligation was suspended, varied or discharged. |
| Prop Data | Expressly named in the original interoperability remedy. I did not establish its individual current compliance status or obtain a verified subsequent instrument varying that obligation. The separate incoming-fee remedy quoted above names the two portals, not Prop Data. |
  
Sources: parliamentary record, subsequent Commission implementation presentation and original remedies summary.   
**Important document limitation:** The Commission publishes the detailed remedial terms in **Annexure 10**, separately from its summary. I could not verify the relevant operative clauses together with any subsequent amendments, settlements, stays or consent orders. The quotation above is therefore **verified original summary wording—not a certified statement of the complete current binding terms**. I found no property-specific subsequent instrument resolving that gap; absence from the material retrieved is not proof that none exists.   
## 2. What Nostoi can claim—and who should authorise the feed  
**My reading is that this is an agency-choice remedy which benefits competing platforms.** The strongest request is:  
**A willing agency instructs its provider to make its authorised marketing listings available to Nostoi through the provider’s compliant interoperability arrangement.**  
I would not characterise this as a right *only* the agency may raise with the Commission. Nostoi can report obstruction itself: the Commission’s complaint process is open to any person. But that procedural entitlement does not replace the agency’s authority to release its listings.   
For your launch, approach **Prop Data as the agency’s existing source-system provider**. Do not frame the request as access to Property24’s or Private Property’s entire aggregated database. Also distinguish free interoperability from an asserted entitlement to a particular bespoke API, additional data enrichment, real-time service levels or unlimited custom development. The wording verified does not establish all those entitlements.  
**Proposed participation-agreement clause**  
This is a drafting starting point for your South African lawyer, not a prescribed Commission form:  
**Listing syndication authority.** The Agency authorises and instructs its nominated listing-management and syndication providers, identified in the Participation Schedule, to transmit to Custom AI Solutions (Pty) Ltd, operating Nostoi, the Agency’s authorised public-marketing listings, together with subsequent updates and withdrawal notifications. The Schedule must identify the participating legal entities, offices and provider account identifiers.  
The Agency grants Nostoi a non-exclusive licence to receive, process, index and display those listings, and to use them for property search, matching and buyer or renter introductions, subject to the content and media rights recorded in this Agreement. Enquiries must be attributed and directed to the relevant listing agency or practitioner.  
Participation does not restrict distribution through any other platform. The Agency may withdraw its syndication authority on written notice, following which Nostoi must promptly stop displaying the affected listings.  
**Send a corresponding signed instruction directly to the provider.** A clause between Nostoi and an agency does not, by itself, amend the provider’s contract or complete its authentication process. Obtain authority from the actual account-holding agency entity; do not assume a franchise brand’s approval covers every independently owned office.  
Keep seller-private information, buyer databases and media lacking documented redistribution rights outside the requested feed.  
## 3. Does an AI advisory platform qualify?  
**In my opinion, Nostoi has a strong functional argument for qualification.** You aggregate agencies’ properties, help consumers select between them and facilitate introductions. An advisory interface rather than a conventional listings grid should not, by itself, defeat that argument.  
The Commission’s newer guidance defines online intermediation functionally, by facilitating transactions between businesses and consumers, and discusses two-sided platforms delivering leads. That supports the interpretation, but **does not determine eligibility under the particular 2023 remedy**. The guidance is expressly non-binding and does not replace the earlier firm-specific decisions.   
For this purpose, describe Nostoi as:  
“A South African online property-intermediation platform providing AI-assisted property search, matching and introductions between home seekers and participating agencies.”  
That is consistent with your product description without pretending to be a conventional classifieds portal.  
I did not identify an OIPMI-specific registration or certification process. Ask the Commission to confirm the applicable eligibility conditions—including any operational-readiness requirements—rather than representing Nostoi as already Commission-approved.  
## 4. Practical enforcement and what has actually happened  
There is useful implementation evidence. The Commission’s later parliamentary presentation says the property interoperability remedies have enabled **MyProperty and Property Central to obtain listings and grow**. That supports treating this as a functioning access route, not merely an unrealised policy aspiration. It does not establish the contractual terms of those connections or complete compliance by every respondent.   
I recommend this sequence:  
1. **Agency-led activation request.** Start with one willing Prop Data agency. Supply its signed instruction, Nostoi’s technical contact, intended uses and security arrangements. Offer to consume the provider’s existing compliant format and build the Nostoi-side adapter. Request a response and implementation timetable within ten business days—expressly your requested timeframe, not a claimed statutory deadline.  
2. **Written explanation of any obstacle.** Ask the provider to distinguish missing agency authority, technical/security requirements and any proposed charges. Request the remedy clause or subsequent instrument on which any refusal or charge relies. Do not immediately equate every technical delay with unlawful obstruction.  
3. **Commission compliance enquiry, then formal escalation if necessary.** Send the correspondence to **++[ccsa@compcom.co.za](mailto:ccsa@compcom.co.za)++**, requesting referral to the OIPMI implementation/monitoring team. Include the agency instruction, chronology, proposed connection, refusal or quotation, and consequences for entry. Specifically request the current Prop Data terms and any variations or relevant orders. The Commission provides both this contact address and a formal complaint process; confidential commercial material should be accompanied by an express confidentiality claim.   
A compliance enquiry is the proportionate first step because you are asking about implementation of an existing remedy. If necessary, competition counsel can frame a prohibited-practice complaint or pursue the appropriate enforcement procedure. Sections 43E and 49B provide complaint-related routes, but neither a complaint nor an allegation of non-compliance automatically produces a supply order or penalty.   
I did not identify a published property-feed penalty or litigated access order that should be represented as a direct precedent for Nostoi.  
## 5. How Nostoi should contract  
Do not assume that every respondent-specific restriction automatically becomes a blanket prohibition applying to Nostoi. Equally, being a new entrant does not make restrictive conduct immune from competition law. The newer guidance highlights interoperability, self-preferencing and misuse of business users’ competitively sensitive information.   
My recommended position is **non-exclusive participation, straightforward termination, no punitive lock-in, no restrictions on rival-platform distribution, and no ingestion charge**. Charge transparently for genuinely optional services, without making payment affect advisory selection or ranking. A voluntary longer-term commercial arrangement is different from a term that effectively prevents agencies supporting competing services.  
**Your eXp role creates a separate trust issue that deserves an explicit contractual answer.** Disclose it and commit to no preferential ranking by network affiliation, no diversion of another agency’s enquiries, and no use of confidential participation or lead data for eXp recruiting or competitive targeting. Back that commitment with access controls, not merely a statement that rankings are unpaid.  
## 6. Wording for agency outreach  
I would use:  
The Competition Commission’s published 2023 property-platform remedies provide for fee-free interoperability enabling agencies to distribute listings to additional platforms.  
We are asking you to authorise Prop Data to add Nostoi as a recipient of your authorised marketing listings through its applicable interoperability arrangement. We will undertake the Nostoi-side technical integration.  
This is an additional, non-exclusive distribution channel. We are not asking you to leave another portal, change your advertising commitments or alter any shareholding. Enquiries about your listings will be directed to your designated listing agent. Nostoi’s advisory rankings are not determined by payment.  
The opening sentence is supported as a statement about the **published 2023 remedies**.  The remaining commitments should appear only if reflected in your agreement and implemented product.  
Do **not** claim that agencies are legally obliged to supply Nostoi; that the Commission has approved Nostoi; that every conceivable feed or integration service must be free; or that all three respondents’ obligations have been confirmed as unchanged and fully implemented today.  
**Bottom line:** Proceed with agency-authorised outreach and a parallel Commission status enquiry. The verified material establishes the original free-interoperability requirement and subsequent real-world access, but does **not** establish the complete current operative terms needed for an unqualified enforcement demand. That is the outstanding verification before counsel sends one.
